# Eltropy Blog Writing Playbook

This is the standing rulebook for every blog written for Eltropy. It captures everything established across our sessions — read this before writing, not just once.

---

## 1. The Core Job

- **Audience is always credit union leaders**, unless explicitly told otherwise.
- **Every blog is built from a resource you're given**: an interview transcript, a case study, a video snippet, an idea, a reference doc. Repurpose it — don't ignore it, and don't publish generic content that could've been written without it.
- **The resource is evidence, not the whole blog** (except for pure Case Study format, where the customer *is* the blog). Find the resource's underlying **theme** — the pain point, the industry trend, the belief being expressed — and make that the spine of the piece. The specific leader name, credit union, or stat gets folded in naturally as proof of the argument, not as the argument's starting point.
- **Never fabricate**: no invented names, titles, credit union names, quotes, or stats. If the resource doesn't state a speaker's title, don't guess — use their name alone and flag the gap to the user before publishing. If a credit union's name is inferred rather than stated outright, flag that too.
- **Priority rule**: when a specific resource's numbers overlap or conflict with general Eltropy product/reference knowledge (see §6), the specific resource always wins. No need to ask — just use it.

---

## 2. Standard Case-Study Structure

Unless a different blog type is specified (see §7), default to this shape:

1. **Industry status** — what's happening broadly in the space (with a verified, linked external stat if one exists)
2. **Pain point** — the generic version of the problem, written so *any* credit union leader recognizes it as their own
3. **The resource as evidence** — introduce the specific credit union/leader naturally, as proof of the pain point and then the fix
4. **Solution** — how the Eltropy product actually solved it, grounded in the resource's specifics
5. **Results** — what changed, in numbers
6. **CTA** — book a demo / talk to us, arrow-styled

Default length: **500–600 words**. In practice, richer case studies with a full FAQ block often run 700–1000 words — that's acceptable, but call out the overage rather than silently blowing past the target.

---

## 3. Voice and Tone Rules

- **Vary the opening every time.** Never default to the same template (e.g., don't always open with a "scene-setting story"). Rotate between a direct question, a sharp observation, a relatable thought. Make it contextual to the specific resource, not formulaic.
- **Write in full, flowing sentences.** No staccato fragments ("New members need help. Wait times spike. Agents scramble."). No rapid-fire "X. Y. Z." lists disguised as sentences.
- **Never use the "it's not X, it's Y" construction.** It's a tic, not a device.
- **Second person throughout.** "You/your," not just in the intro and CTA — carry it through the pain-point section, the solution section, the results section. The reader should feel spoken *to*, not lectured *at*.
- **Confident, not preachy.** State things as true. Don't quiz the reader ("if you've been paying attention...") or imply they're failing. Never moralize.
- **Direct, hooking, conversational.** This is a person talking to a peer, not a brochure.

---

## 4. Structural Requirements (every blog, every time)

- **Headings/subtitles/sections are mandatory.** Even a short 400–500 word thought-leadership piece needs H2s breaking it into sections.
- **At least one bullet list is mandatory**, wherever it earns its place (a pain-point list, a "what changed" list, a checklist).
- **Results/data sections** read as flowing narrative broken into short paragraphs — not one dense block, and not a bare list of stats with no connective tissue (unless the piece is a listicle, where a compact bullet list of stats inside a single numbered item is fine).
- **Quotes always carry full credentials**: name + title + institution. If any of those three is missing from the source material, don't invent it — flag it.

---

## 5. SEO / AEO / GEO Requirements (every blog, every time)

Per the 7-practice framework (intent research, structure for AI discoverability, clarity/authority, contextual CTAs, authoritative links, UX/scannability, differentiated value), every blog includes:

- **SEO Title** and **Meta Description** lines at the top
- **H1 + H2 structure** throughout
- **A CTA at more than one point** — a contextual mid-article CTA (right after a strong quote or pain point) plus the closing CTA, both arrow-styled: `**[Book a demo of Eltropy X](link) →**`
- **A closing FAQ block** that answers zero-click/conversational queries, and **works "Eltropy" into every single question and answer** — this is deliberate, for answer-engine entity association, not just the last FAQ
- **External stats must be real, found via actual search, and linked** with descriptive anchor text plus a `(Source: Publisher)` tag. If the environment's egress restrictions prevent independently re-verifying a link end-to-end, say so plainly rather than presenting it as fully confirmed.
- Skip an external stat entirely rather than use one that can't be sourced.

## 6. The Eltropy AI Voice Reference Knowledge

There's a standing internal reference doc (capabilities, features, implementation process, use cases, and numbers for Eltropy AI Voice/Chat/Assistants) that can be drawn on to explain **how** the product works when a specific case study doesn't spell it out — core integrations (Symitar, Fiserv Portico, CU Answers), authentication methods, generative-AI-vs-scripted distinction, implementation timelines, etc.

Rules for using it:
- It fills gaps; it never overrides a specific case study's numbers (see priority rule in §1).
- Treat its figures as **Eltropy's own stated product facts** — no external link needed, since it's describing Eltropy's own product — not as independently verified third-party research.
- Avoid pricing/ROI figures from it unless the brief specifically calls for a pricing/competitive angle.

## 7. Blog Type Cheat Sheet

Different resources call for different shapes. Ask which type is wanted, or infer it from what the resource actually is, and say which one was picked.

| Type | Reader's question | Structure | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How-To** | "How do I do this?" | Problem → what's needed → numbered steps → mistakes → best practices → next step | Teach |
| **Listicle** | "What should I know?" | Hook → numbered items, each its own mini-argument → conclusion | Organize |
| **Review** | "Is this worth it?" | What it is → who it's for → features → pros → cons → alternatives → verdict | Evaluate |
| **Interview** | "What does this expert think?" | Intro on who/why → edited Q&A (never a raw transcript) → takeaway | Reveal |
| **Newsjacking** | "What does this news mean for me?" | What happened → why it matters → industry implication → what to do | React (only if genuinely relevant — never force the connection) |
| **Case Study** | "Did this actually work?" | Challenge → context → solution → execution → results → quote | Prove |
| **Personal Story** | "What can I learn from this?" | Moment → reaction → shift → lesson → reader takeaway | Connect |
| **Business Announcement** | "What happened?" | What happened → details → why it matters → quote → what's next | Inform |
| **Spotlight** | "Who are the people behind this?" | Person/customer first, company second | Humanize |
| **Thought Leadership** | "What should I think differently about?" | Thesis → current state → why it falls short → your argument → evidence → implications → recommendations | Challenge (needs a genuinely original POV, not a repackaged trend list) |

---

## 8. Listicle-Specific Rules

- Each numbered item gets a **catchy, question-style subheading**, not a flat stat-headline (e.g., "Is Your Chat Channel Quietly Overwhelming Your Team?" not "Chat Volume Dropped 58%").
- Each item should walk through **pain point → impact → how Eltropy fixed it → result** — but this is **not** a rigid label template to reuse every time.
- **Labeled sub-structures (Red Flag: / Questions to Ask Yourself / Our Take:) are a tool, not a default.** Reach for them specifically in evaluative/checklist-style listicles (buyer's guides, "questions to ask before you commit," vendor comparisons) — where the reader's job is to *decide*. For proof-driven listicles (a specific customer's results), let each item read as its own flowing, varied mini-story instead. Don't let any structure — labeled or not — repeat identically across all seven items; vary the rhythm and opening of each one.

---

## 9. Org-Level Guardrails (always in force)

These aren't negotiable and apply regardless of blog type:
- No PII from either Eltropy's customers or their end-users, ever, in any draft.
- No fabricated identities, stats, or synthetic data.
- Financial claims/calculations get flagged as needing independent verification.
- All output must hold up to SOC 2 expectations — nothing here overrides that.

---

## 10. Quick Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Opening isn't a recycled template — it's specific to this resource
- [ ] Second-person voice runs through the whole piece, not just the bookends
- [ ] No fragment sentences, no "not X, it's Y"
- [ ] Headings + at least one bullet list present
- [ ] Every quote has name + title + institution (or a flagged gap)
- [ ] SEO title + meta description included
- [ ] At least one real, linked, sourced external stat (or none, if nothing verifiable exists)
- [ ] Mid-article CTA + closing CTA, both arrow-styled
- [ ] FAQ block present, "Eltropy" in every Q&A
- [ ] Word count checked and any overage from the 500–600 default is called out
- [ ] Any uncertain name/title/institution flagged to the user before this ships
